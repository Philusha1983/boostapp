Privacy Policy

Boost Auto-Book — a free, open-source Chrome extension.
Effective 20 October 2026.
The short version. There is no server. Everything the extension knows lives in your own browser, on your own computer. Nothing is sent to the developer, nothing is sold, and there is no account to create. The extension talks only to your studio's own website — as you, using the session you are already signed in with — and, if you choose to connect one, to your own fitness tracker account.

Who this is from

Boost Auto-Book is an independent, unofficial project by a single developer. It is not made by, affiliated with, or endorsed by Boost / BoostApp (Training Harmony) or any studio. The source code is public, so every claim on this page can be checked: github.com/Philusha1983/boostapp.

What is never collected

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage statistics.
  • No account, sign-up, email address or profile with the developer.
  • No advertising identifiers, and no data sold or shared with anyone.
  • No passwords. The extension never sees or asks for your studio password — it uses the session you created yourself by signing in on the studio's site.

Health data

If you connect a fitness tracker, the extension reads your heart rate, zones, calories and steps for the period around a class you attended, so it can show you that class's heart-rate curve and write the workout to your own health account. This is health information, and it is treated the same as everything else here: it is stored in your browser on your device, it is never sent to the developer, and it is never used for anything other than showing you your own classes.

Connecting a tracker is entirely optional, and the extension's other features — including the muscle map — work without one. You can disconnect at any time in the extension's settings, which removes its access and clears the stored health data.

Who else is involved

When you use the extension, data moves between your browser and these parties only. Each is governed by its own privacy policy, not this one:

  • Your studio's booking site (BoostApp / Training Harmony) — the extension reads your schedule and submits your registrations there, as you. This is the same traffic your browser would send if you clicked the buttons yourself.
  • Your fitness tracker provider, only if you connect one — you authorise access yourself through that provider's own sign-in screen, and the extension never sees your password for it.
  • GitHub, which hosts this page and the extension's downloads, and which receives a request when your browser checks for a new version.
  • Google Fonts, which serves the typeface this page and the extension's interface use.

Permissions, and why each one exists

  • storage — keeps your slots, settings and cached history in your browser.
  • alarms — wakes the extension at the exact moment a registration window opens.
  • notifications — tells you a class was booked, a booking failed, or you have been signed out.
  • cookies — checks only whether your existing session for the studio site is still valid, so auto-booking can pause instead of failing silently. Cookie values are never stored or transmitted.
  • scripting and tabs — read the studio's own rendered pages for your bookings and balance, and open the history dashboard.
  • identity — runs the sign-in flow for the tracker you choose to connect. Unused otherwise.

Your data, your control

  • See it: Settings → Export writes everything the extension stores to a file you can read.
  • Delete it: uninstalling the extension removes all of it. Disconnecting a tracker clears the health data on its own.
  • Move it: Settings → Import restores an exported file on another computer.

Because nothing is stored on a server, there is no account for the developer to delete and no request to file — removing the extension is the deletion.

One limitation worth stating plainly. Data in your browser's local storage is as private as your computer and your browser profile. Anyone with access to your unlocked computer, or to a browser profile you sync, can read it. The extension cannot protect against that, and does not claim to.

Children

The extension is not directed at children and collects nothing that would identify anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version is published on this page with a new effective date, and the change appears in the project's changelog like any other change.

Contact
